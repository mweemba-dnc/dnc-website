What we do

One chain, from a field no bank has ever seen to a balance sheet that can finally reach it.

Four parts of one system. The first one earns today. The rest are what the first one makes possible.

01 · Markets and procurement

A buyer who turns up.

An agent travels to the homestead. The bags are counted, a price is agreed in front of everyone and payment follows before anyone leaves. No waiting on a trader who may never come and no gamble on next week’s price. This is the part of the business that earns, and it is the part that earns the trust everything else depends on.

  • Procurement at the farm gate, not at a distant depot
  • A price agreed in the open and paid on the day
  • Storage, so a harvest can be held rather than dumped
  • The route to market, on infrastructure we do not have to own

02 · Farmer data and Panjiia

Every transaction, recorded and verified.

Volume, price, date and identity, written against a named farmer and reconciled. Captured where the trade happens, offline, and synced when there is signal. A single purchase is a receipt. Four seasons of purchases against the same person is a credit history, and the gap between those two widens every year.

We cannot just jump to saying we have a digital platform and we are collecting data. We have to do the groundwork, the physical labour on the ground, to understand how the system actually works.

Mweemba Ntembe, founder

03 · Finance and inclusion

Trusted data becomes access to finance.

Credit assessed on evidence rather than collateral. Input credit issued before planting and repaid out of the crop rather than in cash. Insurance priced on a real production history. A route into pension and health cover for people no scheme has ever been able to enrol. None of this is live, and each piece arrives only when the record underneath it is good enough to carry it.

Building

Credit before the season

Issued into a farmer’s account, spendable only with approved input suppliers, repaid in grain at the next sale.

Designed

Cover that pays out

Insurance underwritten on a production history that actually exists, rather than on a guess about a district.

In discussion

Pension and health

Enrolment and contribution at the point of sale. Under discussion with national institutions, with nothing yet agreed.

04 · Infrastructure and impact

Permanent capacity for a rural economy.

Storage that stays. Roads that get used. Jobs that are salaried rather than casual. A record that outlasts whoever funded it. The test we apply is simple: if DNC stopped tomorrow, what would be left standing in the district?

One fair purchase does not stop at the farm gate.

One system, built to compound.
